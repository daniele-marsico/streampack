What the numbers mean

  • Integrated LUFS: the average loudness of the whole track, the number platforms use to set the volume
  • True peak (dBTP): the highest level once the waveform is rebuilt between samples. Above -1 dBTP, the MP3 and AAC versions can distort
  • Loudness range (LU): how much the level moves between quiet and loud parts. Low values mean a heavily compressed track

Louder isn't better on streaming

Spotify, YouTube and the other platforms turn loud masters down to their level. A track at -8 LUFS plays about 6 dB quieter on Spotify and keeps the squashed sound of the limiter. Aim near -14 LUFS with true peaks at -1 dBTP or lower, unless you master loud on purpose.

FAQ

How many LUFS should my song be for Spotify?

Spotify plays music at -14 LUFS. A louder master is turned down; a quieter one is turned up only as far as its peaks allow. -14 LUFS with true peak at -1 dBTP is a safe target.

Is this LUFS meter accurate?

It uses the ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighting and gating for integrated loudness, EBU Tech 3342 for loudness range and 4× oversampling for true peak. Results match professional meters within a few tenths of a dB.

Why is true peak higher than the sample peak?

Between two samples the rebuilt waveform can go higher than either of them. Those inter-sample peaks come out when the file is turned into MP3 or AAC, which is why platforms ask for -1 dBTP.
