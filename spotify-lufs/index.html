How Spotify normalization works

  • Target: -14 LUFS integrated (ITU 1770 standard), applied while the track plays
  • Louder masters are turned down to -14 LUFS: no extra distortion, just lower volume
  • Quieter masters are turned up only as far as their peaks allow (Spotify leaves 1 dB of headroom), so a quiet track with high peaks can stay quieter than the rest
  • Spotify's advice: aim for -14 LUFS integrated and keep true peaks below -1 dB

When the -14 LUFS option helps

It helps with demos, live recordings and unmastered bounces that are too quiet or uneven, so they don't sound weaker than the tracks around them. If your song is already professionally mastered, keep the master as it is: the option sets the level, it isn't mastering.

FAQ

Do I have to master at -14 LUFS?

No. Spotify will turn a louder master down anyway. Many engineers master louder on purpose; -14 LUFS is the level you'll be heard at, not a rule.

Does Spotify change my audio file?

No. Spotify measures loudness when the track is uploaded and adjusts the volume during playback.

What do other platforms use?

Most streaming services normalize too, at similar levels. Aiming around -14 LUFS with peaks under -1 dB works well across platforms.
